# Code of Conduct

This document describes the expected behavior in Aevum community spaces. It exists so that people can work together productively and safely — not to create rules for their own sake.

Aevum is an open project. It is built by contributors who choose to participate. The Code of Conduct is short, direct, and intended to be read in a few minutes.

---

## Scope

This Code of Conduct applies to community spaces where people interact **in the context of Aevum**:

- the Aevum GitHub organization (issues, pull requests, discussions);
- Aevum Discord channels;
- community-facing interactions on external platforms where Aevum has a presence (for example, when representing Aevum in a Reddit thread or on X);
- project events, if any are organized.

This Code of Conduct does **not** apply to:

- your personal blog, personal social media, or personal repository;
- personal or private conversations that are unrelated to Aevum;
- technical disagreement expressed in good faith;
- criticism of Aevum, its architecture, or its decisions.

Aevum does not police what people say outside the project. Aevum does expect people to treat each other with respect **inside** the project.

---

## What We Expect

Contributors are expected to:

- **Be respectful.** Disagree with ideas; do not attack people.
- **Be specific.** "This is wrong" is less useful than "This will break if X happens".
- **Assume good faith.** Most disagreements come from different information, not bad intent.
- **Help newcomers.** A person asking a basic question is not a burden. They are the reason the project has a future.
- **Own mistakes.** Being wrong is normal. Correcting it is what matters.
- **Keep it technical when possible.** Not every conversation needs to be deep, but not every conversation needs to be a fight either.

---

## What Is Not Acceptable

The following behaviors are not acceptable in Aevum community spaces:

- **Harassment.** Personal attacks, insults, threats, or targeted hostility.
- **Discrimination.** Slurs, demeaning comments, or exclusion based on identity, background, or belief.
- **Spam.** Repetitive promotional content, unsolicited advertising, or flooding channels.
- **Deliberate disruption.** Repeatedly derailing conversations, ignoring moderation, or acting to provoke.
- **Doxxing.** Publishing private information about another person without consent.
- **Impersonation.** Pretending to be another contributor, a maintainer, or an official Aevum representative.
- **Plagiarism.** Presenting someone else's work as your own in a contribution context.
- **Bad-faith engagement.** Arguing in circles, refusing to engage with evidence, or debating in order to waste others' time.

---

## Three Invariants

These invariants are part of the Code of Conduct and apply alongside the rules above.

### 1. Technical disagreement is not misconduct

Disagreeing with a design decision, a specification, a pull request, or a direction of the project is not a violation.

Aevum is a technical project. Strong technical disagreement is expected. The Code of Conduct applies to **behavior**, not to **positions**.

### 2. No ideological conformity is required

Aevum does not require contributors to share a worldview, a political stance, a religion, or a philosophy.

Contributors are expected to work together on the project, not to agree on everything outside of it.

### 3. Enforcement is proportional and contextual

Moderation is proportionate to the behavior. Not every mistake is a violation, and not every violation requires the same response.

Context matters. A blunt comment in a tense discussion is different from a pattern of targeted hostility. A cultural misunderstanding is different from a slur.

---

## Reporting

If you experience or witness unacceptable behavior, report it.

Current reporting channels are listed on `aevumchain.com` (see the Community page).

Reports are handled with discretion and shared only when necessary to address the situation. Reporters are not required to confront the person involved.

Do not open public issues about Code of Conduct violations. Use the current private reporting channels instead.

---

## Moderation Actions

Moderation actions are proportionate to the behavior.

Typical progression:

- **Warning** — for minor or first-time disruptions.
- **Temporary restriction** — for repeated disruption after a warning.
- **Permanent removal** — for severe or repeated abuse.

Severe abuse (for example, harassment, threats, or doxxing) may justify immediate action without prior escalation.

Moderation is handled by Moderators and Maintainers. A Moderator does not need to be a Maintainer. Moderation is a service, not a technical governance role.

---

## Appeal

If you believe a moderation action against you was incorrect, you may ask for a review.

Appeals go to a different Moderator or to a Maintainer.

Appeals are not a legal process. They are a practical way to correct mistakes.

---

## Governance Boundary

Moderation is about keeping community spaces usable. It is not about deciding what Aevum is.

> **Moderation ≠ technical governance.**

A Moderator may remove spam, harassment, or off-topic abuse. A Moderator may not:

- declare architectural decisions;
- approve or reject protocol changes;
- set the technical direction of the project;
- make or represent technical decisions on behalf of Aevum.

Technical decisions live in GitHub artifacts — issues, pull requests, discussions, and specifications. See **Learn v1 §8** and **Learn v1 §9**.
